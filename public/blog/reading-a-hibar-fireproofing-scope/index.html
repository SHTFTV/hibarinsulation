Reading a HiBar fireproofing scope

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

A clear scope tells the installer and reviewer exactly which areas are included. Read it against the drawings before comparing prices or scheduling access.

Mark the boundaries

Identify floors, structural grids and elements included in the work. Note exclusions and interfaces with adjacent treatments. Broad wording such as all exposed steel may leave questions where services, existing finishes or inaccessible surfaces interrupt the area.

Connect the scope to the design

Ask for the applicable assembly references and requirements to be identified by the design team. Check that proposal assumptions correspond to those documents. Do not resolve a missing design requirement by copying a thickness from a different building.

List the associated work

Include preparation, access, protection, inspections and repairs caused by later trades. Give exclusions a responsible party. A complete scope should show what happens before application and how the finished work is accepted.
