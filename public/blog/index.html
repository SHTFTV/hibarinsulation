HiBar: the product and the assembly

Reading a HiBar fireproofing scope

A clear scope tells the installer and reviewer exactly which areas are included. Read it against the drawings before comparing prices or scheduling access.

Substrate questions before HiBar application

The surface receiving a spray-applied system is part of the specification. Give the project team an opportunity to review its condition and existing treatments before mobilisation.

Assembling a HiBar submittal pack

A submittal should connect the proposed product to the project requirements. Organise it so a reviewer can locate the relevant assembly and identify unresolved questions quickly.

Plan HiBar thickness inspections before work starts

Inspection arrangements should be agreed before the treated surfaces become difficult to access. The design and accepted system documents establish the requirements; an article cannot prescribe a universal application thickness.

Recording HiBar damage after other trades

When later work disturbs spray-applied protection, record the affected locations and arrange a system-specific review. A cosmetic patch should not be assumed to restore the specified assembly.

Commercial access planning for HiBar work

Large overhead areas may require substantial coordination even when their floor area is easy to measure. An access brief helps the contractor price the real working conditions.

Read article →Field notes · 08

HiBar work in an occupied building

Occupation changes how a project is scheduled and handed over. Define the work zones and operational needs before the contractor proposes application arrangements.

Coordinate services around HiBar-treated elements

Services and passive fire protection meet at many points in a building. A coordinated plan helps the project team distinguish different systems and avoid unreviewed changes.

Discussing the appearance of HiBar

Appearance matters in exposed spaces, but visual choices must remain connected to the specified system. A dark or light finish does not establish material identity or fire resistance.

HiBar delivery and material records

Product traceability begins with delivery. A simple receipt and storage routine helps the team identify what reached the site and resolve questions before installation.

Scoping a HiBar repair or replacement review

The size of a visible defect is only one part of a repair decision. Ask the project team to investigate the cause and surrounding condition before deciding how far the work should extend.

The HiBar handover document checklist

A useful handover connects the installed system to its locations and acceptance records. Build the folder during the job so missing information can be resolved before the team disperses.

Compare HiBar tenders on the same scope

A meaningful tender comparison checks the work behind the total. Put each proposal beside one current brief and resolve differences before selecting a contractor.

Photographing HiBar work responsibly

A project image can explain working conditions, but it cannot independently verify a product or rating. Use photographs as part of a traceable project record.

A concise enquiry helps a contractor or technical reviewer identify the next step. Include enough context to distinguish a new installation, a repair assessment and a request for product information.
