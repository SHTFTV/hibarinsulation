Commercial access planning for HiBar work

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

Large overhead areas may require substantial coordination even when their floor area is easy to measure. An access brief helps the contractor price the real working conditions.

Map equipment movement

Show loading points, doors, travel routes and changes in level. Identify fixed equipment and occupied aisles. Have the appropriate project team confirm suitable access equipment and floor restrictions before accepting the setup.

Show overhead congestion

Mark beams, decks, ducts, services and other obstructions on a coordinated plan. State which elements receive the system and which require protection. Ask the contractor to identify areas needing a different access arrangement.

Plan the sequence

Agree working hours, phased releases and return visits. Include access for inspections and corrections as well as application. Assign the responsibility for clearing and handing over each work zone.

Keep with your project brief

  • Delivery and travel routes
  • Overhead coordination plan
  • Phases and inspection access
