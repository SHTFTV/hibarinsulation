Project planning · HiBar Insulation

Coordinate services around HiBar-treated elements

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

Services and passive fire protection meet at many points in a building. A coordinated plan helps the project team distinguish different systems and avoid unreviewed changes.

Identify each system’s role

Show the treated structural elements, services and penetration locations together. Ask the designer to identify any separately specified firestopping or other protection. Do not assume a spray-applied treatment is a substitute for every system at a junction.

Plan work before and after application

Record which supports and service activities must be completed first. Agree how later work will protect the treatment and report damage. Include maintenance access and equipment replacement routes in the discussion.

Keep changes visible

Require altered penetrations, supports and disturbed surfaces to be reviewed through the project’s change process. Retain the accepted detail and any repair record. This gives future trades a usable reference rather than an undocumented patch.

Keep with your project brief

  • Coordinated systems drawing
  • Trade sequence
  • Change and repair records
